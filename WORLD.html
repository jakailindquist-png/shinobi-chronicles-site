Shinobi world atlas

The add-on generates an explorable Naruto-inspired dimension, shinobi:shinobi_world, with 33 named destinations and a fixed geography. The five great villages are built from original Minecraft geometry. Their defining architecture, climates and story locations guide the design; this is a compressed adaptation, not an official map or a block-perfect reconstruction of every anime shot.

The map covers roughly 5,700 blocks east to west and 5,300 blocks north to south around its named locations. Wilderness continues beyond those coordinates. Each location uses the same coordinates in every save, so mission instructions stay reliable. The world seed does not rearrange this atlas. Forest trails connect the central countries; the eastern sea separates island destinations. A full atlas export is bundled at data/shinobi/locations.json.

The three sage regions' exact geographic placements are invented for this playable map. Shikkotsu in particular has limited canonical environmental detail, so its build is explicitly interpretive. Myoboku's association with Jiraiya and the toads is established in the official Jiraiya retrospective. The pack does not claim that these sanctuaries can be reached by these distances or compass bearings in canon.

The large trial clearing exists so huge native tailed-beast entities have room to move. It is not a claim that the nine beasts normally live together or spawn unsealed in one sanctuary. The center at (-550, 83, -2350) has solid ground and open headroom; markers and viewing buildings remain outside the fighting interior. Canon-associated origins and hosts belong to the separate bestiary/campaign material.

Timeline and fidelity limits

This is a persistent adventure stage that brings together Part I and Shippuden landmarks. It does not destroy Konoha when the story reaches Pain, rebuild it later, or erase the valley statues after the final duel. Early-series, pre-Tsunade Konoha has four stylized monument faces. Later characters and missions may be present through the campaign independently of that architectural snapshot.

All buildings, terrain functions and block sculptures are original. No downloaded anime map, ripped game model or copyrighted map image is included. The fidelity priorities are recognizable silhouettes, climate, place names, landmarks and narrative function. Individual house plans, exact distances, most interiors, wilderness topography, the alliance battlefield and shrine layouts are gameplay inventions. Mist uses biome colors and ordinary weather, not volumetric animated fog. The turtle island does not move. The map does not contain a separate Kaguya dimension or Boruto-era city expansion.

Generation and save behavior

ShinobiChunkGenerator runs through Minecraft's normal asynchronous chunk-generation pipeline. It writes only the chunk currently being generated. Terrain, trees and architecture are deterministic functions of world coordinates. Architecture pieces are indexed by chunk, so reaching a village does not run a command that builds the entire country at once.

The generator is registered with Forge as shinobi:atlas; the region-aware biome source uses the same registry name in the biome-source registry. The dimension comes from the bundled datapack JSON. The generator makes its own vegetation and landmarks rather than asking vanilla biome decoration to place villages on top of them. Ordinary mining remains possible through stone, deepslate and deposits of coal, iron, copper, gold, lapis, redstone, diamond and high-altitude emerald. These mineral distributions are gameplay choices.

There is no chunk-load or tick-time rebuilding hook. Once a chunk has generated, edits are normal saved Minecraft changes. Returning to a location does not replace a player's house or regenerate a destroyed village. Changes to this generator affect newly generated chunks only; a new world is the clean way to inspect a revised atlas. Arrival coordinates are initially clear, but later player construction or destructive combat can alter them; travel code should check for safe headroom without rewriting those blocks.

WorldAtlas.LOCATIONS is the stable campaign interface. Each location exposes id, name, x, z, radius and description. WorldAtlas.at(player, id) checks the correct dimension and horizontal discovery radius. WorldAtlas.arrival(id) provides the original safe arrival column. Aliases leaf, sand, mist, cloud, stone, forest and valley resolve to their full destination IDs.

The world GameTests cover unique destination IDs, every planned arrival floor and headroom, distinct landmark materials, climate regions, a clear large-beast arena, actual custom-dimension chunk generation and persistence of a player edit when the chunk is accessed again. These tests do not claim visual review of every building or a restart/unload persistence soak test.
