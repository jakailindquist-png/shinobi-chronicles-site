NPCs, memory, rivals and optional local dialogue

The add-on's six named mission liaisons are original characters using Minecraft's vanilla villager model. They are not animated custom models of Naruto characters. Native Ninshu characters and their built-in interactions remain a separate system. The three original recurring rivals use vanilla vindicator models with explicit rank-scaled health, attack and armor.

Mission liaisons

Use /story contact after choosing a route. Your liaison appears on nearby solid, dry ground with a clear two-block body space, stays for five minutes of world time, and can be moved to another camp after 30 seconds. A newly called liaison retires the previous loaded one; an unloaded old liaison expires when loaded. Liaisons are invulnerable and stationary. Only their owner can open their mission interaction; finishing missions, gifting and free dialogue require the owner to stand within six blocks of the liaison.

Leaf liaison Haru, Sand liaison Saki, Mist liaison Nami, Cloud liaison Ren, Stone liaison Iori and independent liaison Kiri guide different allegiances. They present the current authored briefing and two reporting choices. Both advance the chapter after all objectives are complete:

  • Aid: +8 contact trust and +5 local goodwill.
  • Ambition: +2 trust, +4 village influence and +4 extra mission credits.
  • Share eight bread: +10 trust, with a five-minute cooldown.
  • Insult: −8 trust, with a separate five-minute cooldown.

Trust and reputation clamp between −100 and 100. Shop and travel costs receive a two-credit discount at trust or goodwill 20, and a four-credit discount at 50, with a minimum charge of one. Native NPC trade tables are not overwritten. Village influence is a persistent roleplay statistic; it does not confer authority over every NPC.

Each liaison keeps the latest 12 authoritative event memories, each at most 240 characters. Memories include visits, training, mission choices, supplies shared and rivals defeated. Greetings vary with trust and conversation count, then recall the latest event. These records live in persistent player NBT and are copied on respawn. Inventory still follows the world's inventory rules. The quest ledger and choices are authoritative server data, never generated by a language model.

Shared combat and exploration

Five village routes share chronological encounter phases after their home induction. A player who damages a tagged mission encounter can receive credit when it dies if that player is still on the same phase, at the correct destination, in survival/adventure, in the same dimension and within 128 blocks. Up to 16 contributors are recorded. Players on a different chapter cannot use a repeated native entity to skip ahead. Ordinary wild mobs do not substitute for a tagged mission encounter. Each participant still reports separately and supplies their own delivery.

An active encounter reserves its owner's encounter slot for up to 20 minutes even if its chunk unloads. Death or loaded expiry clears that slot. Mission encounters have a one-minute call cooldown and expire after 20 minutes; they use installed native combat and can be much harder than vanilla mobs. Zabuza and Orochimaru are optional trials: completing one gives 12 extra credits when its chapter is reported, and skipping it gives no bonus. Later story bosses remain required. Choose a broad clearing and prepare native gear and techniques. A missing entity or reward fails safely without consuming mission progress.

Explored locations unlock paid revisits with /story travel <atlas_id>. A return journey checks the generated destination's actual heightmap, solid ground, fluids, world border and two-block body clearance before teleporting or charging credits. A free one-time /story embark at chapter one sets the first safe spawn. None of these commands silently finish a quest.

Recurring rivals

/nemesis opens three persistent original adversaries. Their names are deterministically derived from the character UUID. /nemesis challenge 1, 2 or 3 summons a rival on dry ground inside the shinobi dimension. Challenges have a five-minute cooldown; a reserved rival slot lasts up to ten minutes even across chunk unloading. Combat is disabled in Peaceful and for creative/spectator players.

A rival starts at rank one. Victories over the player increase rank; every two defeats also increase it, capped at rank five. Rank changes health, attack damage and armor. Grudge caps at 100. Defeating a rival records a burn, projectile or melee scar; subsequent fights resist the recorded damage family by 25%. They escape rather than becoming permanently dead, preserving the recurring story. Only their survival/adventure owner earns rivalry victory rewards, so killing a stranger's rival does not farm their credits. Environmental defeat records escape and gives no victory reward.

Once a rivalry has enough grudge, the server can attempt an ambush after ten minutes; it only succeeds at a safe local spawn in the shinobi world. Relevant White Zetsu victories can disrupt rivals' original operations and raise grudge. /nemesis truce requires the rival within 12 blocks and consumes exactly eight gold ingots. It removes the active rival, clears their grudge and records a truce. A new challenge or a relevant provocation can break that truce. These are original rival stories, not claims that canon Akatsuki members repeatedly respawn with new names.

Authored dialogue works immediately

/story say <message> works beside your liaison without installing an AI model. It recognizes mission/destination questions, jutsu or chakra questions, tailed-beast questions, rival questions and memory questions. Everything else receives a relationship-aware authored greeting. Quest claims, training, trades, rewards and rival behavior do not depend on AI.

Optional Ollama mode

Local model dialogue is disabled by default. The first dialogue request creates config/shinobi-ai.json if absent. A host who wants generation can install and run Ollama on the machine hosting the Minecraft world, obtain a suitable model, then set:

{
  "enabled": true,
  "endpoint": "http://127.0.0.1:11434/api/generate",
  "model": "YOUR_INSTALLED_MODEL_NAME",
  "timeoutSeconds": 45
}

The configuration is read per request. The add-on does not download models, install Ollama, use a cloud API, or require an account. In LAN play, localhost refers to the host server, not each guest's computer. Only HTTP loopback endpoints at /api/generate are accepted; credential URLs, query strings, fragments and redirects are rejected. Requests run on a daemon worker, with one generation at a time and a 20-second per-player rate limit. Timeouts, blank output, missing models and HTTP failures fall back to authored dialogue.

The request contains the original NPC name, allegiance, current briefing/objective, trust, the bounded event memory, the latest bounded conversation text and what the player just typed. It does not send the player's UUID, inventory dump, filesystem, account credentials or server log. Conversation history is bounded to the latest 2,200 characters. Player messages are limited to 320 characters; output is capped at 700 characters, removes formatting controls and paired <think> sections, and is marked [AI] in chat.

The system prompt restricts generation to the supplied record, asks for brief in-character replies and forbids invented completed quests, granted powers, future-story revelations or authority over game state. A model can still get details wrong; generated text is decorative conversation. Responses are rendered as literal text and never parsed as commands, rewards, quest completion, inventory mutations or executable code. The server-side mission ledger remains the source of truth.
